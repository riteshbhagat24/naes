This school differentiates itself from others through its philosophy of creating an all-round personality. We aim to nurture young minds and to see them grow into good citizens of our country — and everything we do in the primary and high school years is arranged around that aim.

In practice it means that the school day is deliberately wide. A student here will sit an examination, rehearse for a play, take part in a plantation drive, present at a science exhibition and represent the school on a sports field, often within the same term. None of these is treated as time taken away from studies. They are how a child learns to stand up in front of people, to carry a responsibility to the end, and to work with others who are not exactly like them.

It also means we watch the quieter things. Attendance, handwriting, whether a child has eaten, whether a friendship has gone wrong, whether a strong student has stopped being interested. These are the early signals, and a school that notices them early rarely has to have a difficult conversation late.

My door is open to parents. If something at home is affecting your child's work, tell us; we will adjust. If you think we have got something wrong, tell us that too. A school and a family are on the same side, and the children can always tell when the adults around them are working together.
