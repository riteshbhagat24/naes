Every school is an argument about what matters. Ours was written down before a single classroom was built, and it has not changed since: **love thy neighbour as thyself**. It sits at the centre of our crest, above an open book and a rising sun, and it is the standard against which we still measure the institution.

The late Bishop Adv. Dr. Vincent S. Bower founded this school under the National Apostolic Church Education Society with a conviction that education in South Nagpur should be rigorous and reachable at the same time — that a family should not have to choose between a school that expects a great deal of a child and a school that they can actually afford to send that child to. Alongside him, the late Mrs. Alice Vincent Bower, our Mother Superior, gave the school its second quality: the care that turns an institution into a place a child is glad to arrive at each morning.

Those two instincts — rigour and care — still run the school. They are why we teach every child as an individual rather than as a row in a register. They are why our teachers are asked to know each student's pace, and why a good result is never the only result we discuss with a parent. And they are why the work of the school does not stop at the examination: the plantation drives, the exhibitions, the stage, the playing field and the community days are not decoration around the curriculum, they are part of it.

Today the Society oversees a full pathway of education on a single campus — Pre-Primary through Class XII at New Apostolic English High School, and Junior College and degree programmes at Dr. Bower Apostolic Junior College. A child can arrive here at three years old and leave with a degree, taught throughout by people who have watched them grow up.

Our responsibility to the families of Nagpur is to keep that pathway honest: to invest in the classrooms and laboratories, to hold our teaching to account, and to send out young men and women who are capable, hard-working and decent. That is the whole of the founding vision. It has proved to be enough.
